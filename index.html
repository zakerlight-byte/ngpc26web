YOUR QUICK GUIDE

MUN, in brief.

Know the format. Then check the conference rules for your committee.

01

GSL

The General Speakers List is the main queue for formal speeches. Share your delegation’s position, explain why it matters, then offer one practical next step.

Try this: “Honourable Chair, [country] believes… We are concerned because… We encourage the committee to…”

Confidence tip: Write your first two sentences, breathe before you begin, and look up once during the speech. You do not need to sound perfect; make one clear point.

02

Position paper

A short paper explaining the issue, how it affects people, your country’s position, and the solutions it supports. Keep it tied to your committee’s actual agenda.

Build it: 1) issue and impact, 2) your country’s policy or past action, 3) two or three realistic proposals. Add reliable sources.

Confidence tip: Turn each section into a few bullet points first. Your paper is a preparation tool, not a test of fancy wording.

03

Draft resolution

A group plan the committee negotiates. Preambulatory clauses explain the problem and relevant context; operative clauses describe specific actions.

Make it useful: Say who will act, what they will do, and how. Start with a few achievable ideas, then invite other delegates to improve them.

Confidence tip: You can contribute by researching, writing one clause, asking a question, or helping the group agree. You do not have to lead every conversation.

CAUCUSES

In a moderated caucus, the chair recognizes short speeches on one focused question. In an unmoderated caucus, delegates talk freely, find partners, and write together. Ask the chair to clarify the rules if you are unsure; that is completely okay.
